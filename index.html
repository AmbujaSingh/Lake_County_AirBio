    About This Project

    About & Disclaimer

    What this project is

    Lake County AirBio is an independent, student-developed environmental awareness project focused on Lake County, Illinois. It consolidates daily pollen, mold, air-quality, and weather conditions into a single view and tracks how these measures change over a seven-day period, with the aim of helping residents recognize patterns that may relate to common allergy symptoms.

    Purpose and limitations

    This website is an awareness and pattern-analysis tool. It is not a medical device and does not provide diagnosis, treatment, or emergency guidance. Environmental sensitivity varies considerably between individuals, and the relationships described on this site are general associations rather than predictions for any one person. Residents experiencing severe or recurring symptoms should consult a licensed healthcare professional.

    How the risk score is calculated

    The daily Allergy Risk score is a weighted estimate: tree pollen (30%), mold risk (20%), grass pollen (15%), air quality (15%), humidity (15%), and weed pollen (5%). Rainfall and wind speed are treated as secondary, contextual factors that help explain — but do not directly score — the mold and pollen figures. The result is reported as Low, Moderate, or High, along with a plain-language explanation of the leading contributors for that day.

    Data & methodology

    Temperature, humidity, wind speed, rainfall, and AQI are real figures for each of the 47 municipalities plotted on the map — not just the county seat. A scheduled process fetches them once a day from Open-Meteo's free weather and air-quality APIs, which blend national weather-service models, including NOAA/NWS, for the United States; the current US AQI formula is used for air quality. Pollen and mold indices remain modeled: no free, public API publishes county-level pollen counts, so these figures are produced by a documented heuristic based on the calendar month and each city's recent humidity and rainfall, clearly labeled as an estimate rather than a measurement. A future upgrade path exists to a licensed pollen feed, such as the National Allergy Bureau, or to the EPA's AirNow program as an alternative AQI source, without changing how the site displays the data.
